## 3. Introduction

**Artificial Intelligence (AI)** is a broad field of computer science concerned with creating systems that can perform tasks associated with intelligent behavior.

These tasks can include:

- Reasoning
- Problem solving
- Learning
- Planning
- Perception
- Language understanding
- Decision-making

A simple representation is:

```text
Human Intelligence
       ↓
Algorithms + Data + Computing
       ↓
Artificially Intelligent System
       ↓
Intelligent Task
```

AI is a broad concept. It can include systems based on explicit rules as well as systems that learn from data.

The key idea is:

> **Artificial Intelligence aims to build machines or software systems that can perform tasks requiring aspects of intelligent behavior.**

# 4. What is Artificial Intelligence?

## Definition

**Artificial Intelligence is the field of creating computational systems capable of performing tasks that involve capabilities such as perception, reasoning, learning, planning, language understanding, and decision-making.**

The exact definition of AI can vary, but the common idea is that machines perform tasks associated with intelligence.

Examples include:

```text
Speech Recognition
Image Understanding
Game Playing
Planning
Recommendation
Language Processing
Decision Support
```

---

# 5. Main Characteristics of AI

AI systems may demonstrate one or more of the following capabilities:

### Learning

The system can improve performance using data or experience.

### Reasoning

The system can use information and logical procedures to reach conclusions.

### Problem Solving

The system can search for or construct solutions to a problem.

### Perception

The system can interpret information from sources such as images, audio, sensors, or text.

### Language Understanding

The system can process or generate human language.

### Decision-Making

The system can select actions based on goals, information, or learned patterns.

Not every AI system has all of these capabilities.

# 6. Types of Artificial Intelligence

A common conceptual classification is:

```text
Artificial Intelligence
       │
       ├── Narrow AI
       │
       └── General AI
```

## 6.1 Narrow AI

**Narrow AI**, also called weak AI, is designed for a specific task or limited set of tasks.

Examples include:

- Spam detection
- Recommendation systems
- Image classification
- Voice assistants
- Chess-playing programs

A system may be extremely effective at one task without possessing general human-like intelligence.

Most practical AI systems in use today fall into this category.

---

## 6.2 General AI

**Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)** is a concept referring to a machine with broad, general-purpose intellectual abilities across many different tasks.

An AGI would be expected to transfer knowledge and skills across a wide variety of problems rather than being limited to one narrow function.

AGI remains a research and conceptual goal rather than a standard capability of current deployed AI systems.

# 8. AI and Rule-Based Systems

AI does not always require Machine Learning.

A system can be designed using explicit rules.

Example:

```text
IF temperature > threshold
AND sensor indicates abnormal behavior
THEN raise an alert
```

The rule is written directly by a human.

This is different from a Machine Learning model, where the system learns relationships from data.

Therefore:

```text
AI
├── Rule-Based Approaches
└── Learning-Based Approaches
       └── Machine Learning
             └── Deep Learning
```

This is a simplified conceptual view.

---

# 9. AI vs Machine Learning vs Deep Learning

These terms are related but not identical.

| Concept | Main Idea |
|---|---|
| Artificial Intelligence | Broad field of intelligent machine behavior |
| Machine Learning | Systems learn patterns from data |
| Deep Learning | Neural-network-based approach within ML |

A useful hierarchy is:

```text
Artificial Intelligence
          ↓
   Machine Learning
          ↓
      Deep Learning
```

This hierarchy describes a common subset relationship, not every possible AI method.

# 17. Advantages of Artificial Intelligence

### Automation

AI can automate certain repetitive or complex tasks.

### Speed

Computational systems can process large quantities of information quickly.

### Pattern Recognition

AI and ML systems can identify patterns in large datasets.

### Decision Support

AI can provide predictions, recommendations, or other information to support decisions.

### Scalability

Software-based AI systems can process large numbers of cases once appropriately deployed.

---

# 18. Limitations and Challenges

### Data Quality

Learning-based AI systems depend heavily on the quality of their data.

### Bias

AI systems can reproduce or amplify biases present in data or system design.

### Explainability

Some complex models are difficult to interpret.

### Privacy

AI applications may process sensitive or personal information.

### Security

AI systems can be vulnerable to misuse, attacks, or unreliable inputs.

### Reliability

An AI system can produce incorrect outputs, especially outside the conditions for which it was designed and evaluated.

### Cost

Training and operating advanced AI systems can require significant computing and engineering resources.

# 19. Human Intelligence vs Artificial Intelligence

AI and human intelligence should not be treated as identical.

| Aspect | Human Intelligence | Artificial Intelligence |
|---|---|---|
| Learning | Broad and flexible | Depends on system and training |
| Generalization | Strong across many contexts | Often limited to designed capabilities |
| Common Sense | Developed through broad experience | Can be limited or unreliable |
| Emotions | Human biological and social processes | Can simulate certain responses |
| Speed | Limited by human processing | Can process large amounts of data quickly |
| Adaptability | Broad | Depends on system design |

AI systems can outperform humans in specific tasks without having general human-like intelligence.

Data Science

AI

# 21. Responsible Use of AI

AI systems should be developed and used carefully.

Important considerations include:

- Fairness
- Privacy
- Security
- Transparency
- Reliability
- Human oversight
- Accountability

The appropriate safeguards depend on the application and its potential impact.

A technically accurate model may still be unsuitable if it is used in an unsafe or inappropriate way.
